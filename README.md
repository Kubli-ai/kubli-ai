What we do

Companies hold data they cannot use: locked up by privacy, regulation, or the risk of recognising someone. Kubli makes it usable and attaches the measurement of the remaining risk, together with what that measurement does not cover.

In 2024 the European regulator wrote down that two datasets looking alike prove nothing about anonymity. The evidence is trying to break the result and measuring whether you can. That is the part we build.

Four modules, one platform

The modules are independent: switch on the one you need, the others do not run and are not on your invoice.

Module	What it does
Echo	Anonymises archives into a usable copy: same columns, same shape, different people. Risk is measured with the three attacks the EDPB names (singling out, linkability, inference). Above the threshold, the file is not released.
Pulse	Flags anomalies in recurring flows (payments, claims, utility usage, returns) before the operation completes. Learns from your own closed cases, made usable by Echo.
Compass	Records what every automated decision about a person was based on, as it is made. Produces the documentation the EU AI Act requires for high-risk systems, including differences in behaviour between groups.
Sentinel	Anonymises access and system logs so you can keep, share and analyse them, then surfaces only what departs from a normal day on your network.
How it works
It integrates, it does not replace. Sits alongside your existing systems: no migration, no rewriting.
Light by design. One executable, nothing to install, no GPU. Sources are read in blocks, so memory does not grow with data volume.
It runs where you decide. On our infrastructure, or inside your own network on Team and Enterprise. In the second case the rows never cross the perimeter: only counts and agent status do.
Every result carries its measurement. Alongside the outcome comes the document reporting how it was obtained and what the measurement does not cover.

What we are not

Kubli holds no third-party certifications and issues no compliance stamps. Thresholds are prudent conventions with EDPB rationale, not numbers set by law, and every report says so. Treated data does not automatically become anonymous under the GDPR: what we hand over is a defensible position, not a guarantee.
